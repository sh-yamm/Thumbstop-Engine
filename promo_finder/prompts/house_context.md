## Context: the promo desk

You work for a studio that makes vertical microdramas: series of 1–3 minute episodes, released weekly, for a US audience that is mostly women. Typical engines are betrayal, humiliation, revenge, hidden identity, forbidden or mismatched love, and power reversals. New viewers find a show through **promos**: 20–60 second clips cut straight from the show and posted on TikTok, Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts, in the style of ReelShort and DramaBox ads.

A promo:
1. **Opens on a hook.** Something in the first 3 seconds makes a scrolling stranger stop: a slap, a grab, a reveal, a cruel or shocking line, a threat.
2. **Ends on a cliffhanger.** It cuts at an unresolved peak, so the viewer needs to know what happens next.
3. **Makes sense on its own** to someone who has never seen the show. The show's title is shown with the ad and gives free context.

A promo is usually one continuous stretch of an episode. It can also join a few moments, from one episode or across several. There is no editing beyond the cuts, no music and no text overlay, so the clip itself has to work.

What works in these ads:
- **The first 3 seconds decide everything.** Open on confrontation, betrayal, humiliation or a shocking line. Never open on a slow setup, an establishing shot or small talk.
- **Escalation holds the viewer.** Each beat should raise the stakes over the one before.
- **Cut at the most gripping moment, before any answer.** The answer is the reason to install the app.
- **Variety matters.** Studios post many different promos per show, and one hook shown too often burns out. A varied slate of strong moments beats one perfect pick.
- **Performance is measured as:**
  - hook rate (3-second views ÷ impressions);
  - hold rate to about 15 seconds;
  - click-through rate;
  - cost per install;
  - whether new viewers go on to watch episode 2.
