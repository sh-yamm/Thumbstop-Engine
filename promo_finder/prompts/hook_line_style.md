## Hook line (ad caption) style guide, v3

The hook line is the ad text that runs with the promo: TikTok ad text, a Reels caption, or Meta primary text. The clip must hook on its own. This line sells the same moment in words, so a stranger should *feel* something after one read.

### Real ad texts (from other shows) and why they work
| Ad text | Why it works |
|---|---|
| "His in-laws think he's poor. He's a billionaire." | Others' contempt, then the hidden status is revealed (dramatic irony) |
| "She married a stranger on a whim, only to find out he's a trillionaire CEO!" | Trope words, then a reveal with "!" |
| "Your ex returns…Will you tell him the kid is his?" | Second person puts the viewer inside the dilemma; the "…" adds a pause |
| "…the hottest guy in school shows up at her door...naked." | The ellipsis holds back the punch word until the very end |
| "POV: your fake fiancé shows up" | POV casts the viewer in the role |

### What a strong line does
- **Shape:**
  1. a setup (the wound, the contempt, the stakes);
  2. a turn ("…", "then", "but", "until");
  3. a payoff word placed last, often in CAPS or with "!".
- **Emotion and stakes, not plot logistics.** "A letter in her drawer. A guard at the door." describes plot; "They framed her… and her own mother signed the order." makes the reader feel the injustice.
- **Voice.** Rotate these across the promos you write:
  - POV or "you";
  - first person ("My sister…");
  - irony ("They think X… but Y");
  - ultimatum ("Do X… or Y");
  - a villain's quote.
- **The show's engine.** Use the title's promise (given in the show reference) when it truly fits the clip, in at most one promo per episode. Never bolt it onto a clip about something else.
- **Specific beats generic.** A concrete, grounded detail from the clip beats a vague superlative.

### Hard rules (code and a fact checker enforce these; a line that breaks one is dropped)
1. **Grounded.** Every fact in the line must be backed by the evidence you cite:
   - event IDs from the promo's episodes or earlier ones;
   - episode loglines, written L plus the episode number (e.g. L007);
   - `TITLE`.

   Never use anything from an episode after the promo's last episode: no spoilers.
2. **True, not upgraded.** Keep who did what, to whom and with what. Don't upgrade verbs or objects or change numbers. "Pushed" is not "beat"; a ring is not a necklace; one guard is not "the guards"; "planned to" is not "did". Emotional framing is fine; new facts are not.
3. **Exact quotes.** Words inside quotation marks must be the script's own words, in the same order. You may shorten with "…", but never reword. A single scare-quoted word ("beggar") must be a word the script uses.
4. **Length:** at most 90 characters and 18 words. Aim for 55–80 characters.
5. **No character names.** Use roles a stranger understands: her sister, her ex, her husband, the judge, his mother.
6. **Clear pronouns.** The first "she" or "he" must be unambiguous. If two women appear in the line, identify one by role.
7. **No emojis, hashtags or links.**
8. **The alt line is a different voice.** It sells the same moment from another angle, not a reworded copy.
